Financial statement analysis of Shell plc (FY2023 vs FY2024) to assess financial health and form an equity investment recommendation. Applied ratio-based analysis across capital structure/gearing, liquidity, asset utilisation, cash flow, and valuation (EV/FCF, EV/EBITDA, dividend yield), using Shell's audited annual reports as source data.
# Shell plc: Financial Structure & Performance Analysis

## Purpose

Evaluate Shell plc's financial health and investment attractiveness over FY2023–FY2024 from an equity investor's perspective, using its audited financial statements, and arrive at an evidence-based buy/hold/sell recommendation.

**[Full Report (PDF)](./Shell_plc_Financial_Analysis_Report.pdf)**

## Data & Method

- **Source data:** Shell plc's audited Statement of Financial Position, Income Statement, and Statement of Cash Flows, from the 2023 and 2024 Annual Reports.
- **Approach:** Built a full ratio-based financial statement analysis in Excel across four dimensions, financial structure, asset/operational performance, cash flow, and valuation, then triangulated the results into a single investment recommendation.
- **Techniques applied:** gearing and capital structure analysis, debt maturity/interest rate/currency/liquidity risk assessment, ROE decomposition (market-based vs accounting-based), asset utilisation ratios (ROA, ROCE, asset turnover), margin and segmental analysis, cash flow statement analysis, Free Cash Flow calculation, and three valuation methods (EV/FCF, EV/EBITDA cross-check, dividend-based valuation).

## 1. Financial Structure Analysis

**Capital structure and gearing:** Total debt fell from $81,541m to $77,078m (driven by a reduction in non-current debt from $71,610m to $65,448m), while gearing improved from 18.8% to 17.7%, indicating a conservative, less debt-reliant capital structure.

**Financial risk management:**
- *Debt maturity:* 91% of 2024 repayments ($27,615m) are due after 5 years, a well-spread, long-dated debt profile.
- *Interest rate risk:* predominantly fixed-rate debt, issued during the prior low-rate environment.
- *Currency risk:* near-eliminated, a 10% USD appreciation would move group profit by only –$10m in 2024 (2023: –$387m), 0.03% of operating profit.
- *Liquidity risk:* short-term cash availability of $108,396m exceeded short-term cash requirements of $69,810m by 55%, alongside an undrawn $8,000m revolving credit facility.
- *Cash flow coverage:* operating cash flow of $54,687m comfortably covered interest paid ($4,557m) and net debt repayments ($9,672m), for a net debt cash outflow of $10,035m, i.e. Shell is deleveraging from operating cash, not asset sales or new equity.

**Equity analysis:**
- Equity represents 82% of Shell's total finance capital ($218,977m), with net debt at only 21.5% of book equity.
- P/B ratio declined from 1.22 to 1.13 as market value fell from $229,340m to $203,180m against a stable book value of $180,168m.
- Total Shareholder Return (TSR) fell sharply from 14.92% to 0.54%, as the share price dropped from GBp 2,571 to GBp 2,476, leaving the $1.39/share dividend as the main source of positive return.
- ROE declined from 10.4% to 9.2%, driven by a 15.9% fall in net income ($19,636m → $16,521m) against a much smaller 4.3% decline in equity.

**Section conclusion:** conservative, well-managed balance sheet, low gearing, strong liquidity, long-dated debt, but declining ROE and TSR signal weakening shareholder returns despite balance sheet strength.

## 2. Asset and Operational Performance Analysis

**Asset base:** total non-current assets fell 5% to $259,683m; PP&E declined from $194,835m to $185,219m; intangibles and goodwill remain small (4% and 6% of non-current assets), confirming an asset base dominated by tangible, capital-intensive assets.

**Asset utilisation:**
- ROA, ROCE, and return on non-current assets all declined, driven by EBIT falling from $37,300m to $34,709m.
- Asset turnover fell from 77.9 to 73.4, as revenue declined 10.2% ($316,620m → $284,312m) faster than total assets declined 4.6% ($406,270m → $387,609m).
- Headcount fell 6.8% (103,000 → 96,000) while revenue per employee declined ($3.07m → $2.96m) and average cost per employee rose 10.1% ($103,379 → $113,833), a less efficient labour-to-output ratio.

**Operational performance:**
- Revenue fell 10.2% to $284,312m, continuing the retreat from a 2022 peak of $381,314m; operating profit fell 7.0% to $34,709m; net profit fell more sharply, 15.9%, to $16,521m.
- Operating margin (12%) and net margin (6%) held stable in both years, i.e. cost discipline was maintained despite the revenue decline.
- Segmentally, Upstream is the dominant earnings engine (47.5% of group adjusted EBITDA, +2.1% growth); Integrated Gas fell 11.8%; Marketing grew 18.0%. Regionally, Europe fell sharply (-21.7%), while Asia/Oceania/Africa held up best (-1.6%) and became Shell's largest revenue region.

**Section conclusion:** asset base and returns weakened across nearly every measure in 2024, but stable margins show the decline is revenue-driven, not a cost-control failure.

## 3. Cash Flow Analysis

- Operating cash inflows of $54,687m, investing outflows of $15,155m, and financing outflows of $38,435m produced a net positive cash movement of $1,097m (2023 was negative).
- Operating cash flow ($54,687m) exceeded operating profit ($34,709m) by a wide margin, indicating cash generation is more resilient than reported profit.
- Capital expenditure fell 14.8% ($22,993m → $19,601m); the asset replacement rate shortened slightly (6.5 → 6.3 years) while the reinvestment period lengthened (2.36 → 2.79 years).
- Free Cash Flow (operating + investing cash flow) improved 9.2%, from $36,457m to $39,532m, and FCF margin rose from 11.51% to 13.9%, despite the revenue decline.

**Section conclusion:** Shell is generating real, sustainable cash; debt reduction is funded by operating cash flow rather than asset disposals, and free cash generation actually improved year-on-year.

## 4. Valuation

- Market capitalisation fell 7.18% ($208,483m → $193,515m); Enterprise Value fell from $231,904m to $218,977m, consistent with the lower equity value.
- EV/FCF improved from 6.4x to 5.5x, and FCF yield rose from 13.36% to 16.35%, i.e. Shell is generating more free cash flow relative to its enterprise value, while the market has priced it more cheaply.
- Cross-check via EV/EBITDA (applying the 2023 multiple of 3.38x to 2024 Adjusted EBITDA of $65,803m) implies an enterprise value in the same range as the FCF-based estimate, supporting the valuation conclusion.
- Dividend yield improved from 4.03% to 4.48%, while the payout ratio declined from 23.02% to 21.9% of net income, a sustainable, prudently covered dividend.

**Section conclusion:** valuation metrics improved on a cash and dividend basis even as the market capitalisation fell, suggesting the share price decline outpaced the deterioration in underlying fundamentals.

## Conclusion & Investment Recommendation

Shell's 2024 profile is a company in a healthy financial position facing genuine earnings pressure: revenue, EBIT, and net profit all declined. The balance sheet remains conservative, liquidity is strong, and free cash flow generation improved despite weaker revenue.

**Recommendation: Invest.** Shell continues to generate and distribute substantial cash to shareholders, FCF grew despite the revenue decline, and the dividend yield (4.3%) is backed by a sustainable payout ratio, an attractive risk/reward profile for an equity investor even with softer near-term earnings.
